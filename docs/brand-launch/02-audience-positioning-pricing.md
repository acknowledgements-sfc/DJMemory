# DJMemory Audience, Positioning, and Pricing

Last updated: August 13, 2026.

## Primary launch audience

**Recording-active Mac DJs using Serato or rekordbox who play at least monthly and do not have a dependable post-set archive habit.**

This audience is narrow enough to recognize the problem immediately, large enough to recruit from the existing community, and closely aligned with the product's strongest verified workflows.

## Audience hierarchy

1. **Primary:** Club and event DJs who record live sets at least monthly on a Mac.
2. **Secondary:** Radio, streaming, and mix-series DJs who need a reliable history of finished sessions.
3. **Tertiary:** Touring and multi-platform DJs whose recordings and histories are fragmented across software and hardware.
4. **Later:** Occasional hobbyists, Windows-first DJs, and teams managing multiple artists.

## Provisional behavioral personas

These are recruitment hypotheses. Replace assumptions with interview evidence after 8–12 conversations.

### Maya — the working club DJ

- Plays two to four times per month using rekordbox and occasionally Serato.
- Records when the booth allows it, then leaves files in whichever folder the software chose.
- Wants a dependable record of her growth without another task after a late set.
- Trigger: recently looked for a strong set and could not find the recording.
- Objection: does not want background software touching her library.

### Chris — the mobile and event DJ

- Plays weekly, often on his own controller and laptop.
- Has long recordings with valuable transitions, client moments, and reusable material.
- Values reliability and plain proof over a creative lifestyle pitch.
- Trigger: needs a consistent record of gigs and a safer workflow than copying files manually.
- Objection: assumes existing cloud backup already covers the problem.

### Nia — the radio and content DJ

- Records regular shows and guest mixes, then prepares uploads and tracklists.
- Needs audio, date, show context, and history to stay connected.
- Trigger: publishing backlog caused by scattered files and missing context.
- Objection: may see DJMemory as redundant with a DAW or station archive.

### Andre — the multi-platform touring DJ

- Moves between Serato, rekordbox, club hardware, and guest setups.
- Cares about consistency across tools and retaining control of unreleased music.
- Trigger: fragmented set history across machines and software.
- Objection: needs confidence that “supported” means tested in his exact setup.

## Value proposition

**DJMemory automatically protects completed DJ set recordings on your Mac, keeps them organized with useful session context, and leaves every source file untouched.**

## Positioning statement

For active DJs who record performances but cannot rely on a perfect post-set filing habit, DJMemory is a local-first set protection app that automatically preserves completed recordings and builds a usable history of the work. Unlike cloud library products, production suites, or manual recording setups, DJMemory focuses on the finished set and works without taking custody of the artist's audio.

## Message hierarchy

1. **Why care:** A finished set can disappear into the wrong folder—or never become part of your history.
2. **Solution:** DJMemory quietly preserves completed recordings and keeps them findable.
3. **Why DJMemory:** It is local-first, copy-only, and designed around DJ workflows across software.
4. **Action:** Join the private beta and help test it with real sets.

## Reasons to believe

- Real Serato and rekordbox recordings have been archived successfully.
- The source file remains unchanged; DJMemory creates a protected copy.
- Every archive receives a metadata sidecar and can be enriched with set context.
- Local protection does not depend on an account.
- Audio and full tracklists are not uploaded by default.
- Compatibility labels distinguish supported, partial, and manual workflows.

## Benefit-territory test

Show each interview participant these three unlabeled concepts in rotating order:

1. **Never lose a completed set.** “DJMemory quietly protects the recording when your set is over.”
2. **Build a history of your work.** “Every set becomes part of an organized archive you can return to.”
3. **One protection layer across DJ apps.** “Keep the same archive habit even when your DJ software changes.”

Ask which is most valuable, most believable, and most likely to earn a beta install. Record the words participants use before showing polished language.

## Pricing recommendation

Do not charge during invite-only beta. After retention is demonstrated, test this provisional model:

| Tier | Price to test | Includes | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Beta | Free | Full local protection during testing | Remove purchase friction and maximize learning |
| DJMemory Core | $49 one-time | One Mac, folder protection, archive, session context, manual history attachment | Match the mental model of a dependable Mac utility |
| DJMemory Plus | $79 one-time | Core plus app-audio capture, input capture, advanced export/publish packs, priority compatibility updates for 12 months | Capture higher value without gating the core promise |
| Major upgrade | $24–$39 optional | Future major-version features; existing version continues working | Fund continuing development without rent on local files |

Why this is the leading hypothesis:

- Cloud competitors justify subscriptions with storage and ongoing hosted services; DJMemory's central job is local.
- General-purpose recording is free, so the paid value must be automation, trust, and DJ-native organization.
- Density demonstrates an accepted one-time/rent-to-own frame for DJ recording software.
- A perpetual option reduces subscription resistance visible in DJ-library communities.

Validate with Van Westendorp-style questions after participants understand the working product: too cheap to trust, good value, getting expensive, and too expensive. Also ask whether ongoing compatibility updates change their preference between one-time and subscription pricing.

## Claims to avoid

- “Never lose a set” as an absolute technical guarantee. Use “protect every completed recording” or “a safer home for every set.”
- “Works with every setup.” Show exact support status instead.
- “Backup” without explaining that the default archive can still share the same physical disk.
- “Invisible” or “zero setup.” Folder permissions and support differences must remain clear.
- Any claim that DJMemory records streaming-service content regardless of licensing restrictions.
